Proposal · not applied to the map

Retain the varied warm neutrals and restrained blue-green glass. Correct known building materials and reduce the orange outliers locally, while preserving the existing light and atmosphere.

The main fix is material assignment.

Wells Fargo North and KPMG currently share OSM color #9C5B31, exported as orange-brown #a06038 with a glass material. Use muted rose granite for the cladding, with separate dark bronze glazing. A global desaturation would also dull buildings that already work.

These are palette inputs shown as flat sRGB swatches, not rendered previews. The final map will be affected by lighting, glass reflections, tone mapping, and 4-step channel quantization.

Scope and confidence

First pass: Wells Fargo North / KPMG orange response; 777 and Bank of America cladding; Aon dark bronze; Gas Company stone shaft and blue glass crown. KPMG's matching treatment is a complex-level inference from Wells Fargo Center evidence and should be checked against a close reference before implementation. Chase / BNY Mellon also carries the orange source tag: reduce its chroma provisionally, but verify its cladding before assigning the Wells Fargo material.

Small alignment only: City Hall, Broad, Disney, Union Station, U.S. Bank and Wilshire already have explicit landmark colors. Keep their established facade patterns and nighttime behavior. Two California Plaza is provisional: cached OSM establishes glass, but its proposed hue needs better photographic corroboration.

Photo sampling: Four reference regions were sampled as median decoded sRGB pixels, without white balance or lighting correction. The Bank of America sample is a clipped sunlit cap; the Broad sample is an evening mix of concrete and recesses. Neither should set the base color. Aon and Disney samples demonstrate sky reflections and likewise must not be copied directly into physical material color. Primary material descriptions are the stronger evidence.

Geometry remains separate: Color will improve Disney's steel appearance but cannot recover its overlapping curved shells. The existing roof-height correction remains an approximation.

Implementation and review sequence

  1. Resolve verified building identity before material/color tags and generic fallback.
  2. Separate opaque cladding from glazing for mixed facades; color alone will not fix orange glass response.
  3. Replace universal dark-color lightness floor with material-specific appearance policy.
  4. Normalize facade chroma in a perceptual color space before palette quantization; preserve hue and perceptual lightness where feasible.
  5. Keep existing global lighting, vegetation, roads, and generic palette variety; soften only excessive orange chroma.
  6. Re-export all LODs and compare identical day, sunset, and night cameras; preserve Broad illumination, Wilshire plain spire, and obstruction lights.

Suggested initial OKLCH chroma ceilings: stone/stucco and glass/bronze 0.035–0.05; terracotta 0.07–0.085; steel 0.015. Tune against fixed-camera renders rather than treating these as measured material constants. Keep roof terracotta distinct from the unwanted orange tower cladding.

Acceptance: no neon-orange tower faces; white towers remain recognizable; Aon stays dark without crushing detail; pale landmarks remain distinct; daytime, sunset, and nighttime views retain their current mood and lighting corrections.
