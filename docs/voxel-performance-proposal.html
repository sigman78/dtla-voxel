Render less, retain less, prepare static work once

This is the pre-implementation research record. Implementation decisions, validation and updated measurements are tracked in the feature plan and re-evaluation.

Start with an unused attribute byte and compact query data, then replace full-city fixed-detail loading with bounded, screen-space-driven residency. Make compact quad rendering the next major experiment. GPU occlusion should come after those reductions and must demonstrate a net saving.

What the code and experiments confirm

The viewer already does useful work: worker-side greedy face meshing with AO and material-aware merges; hidden-neighbor face removal except deliberately closed tile borders; four indexed vertices per quad; 16-bit integer positions; compact normal/AO/ground/palette attributes; transferable worker replies; shared quad indices; CPU attribute release after upload; Three.js per-mesh frustum culling; idle worker retirement; and invalidated shadow-map caching. These are baseline features, not proposed optimizations.

Bound the active world, then choose its detail

Separate four needs

Maintain separate sets for color/AO rendering, shadow casters, query data and prefetch/cache. A tile outside the camera frustum may still cast a visible shadow or be required by a raycast. Setting mesh.visible=false based solely on the main camera would also remove its shadows. Use pass-specific frustum/selection rules and a conservative union for residency.

Keep a small coarse city fallback, request visible fine tiles plus a narrow predictive margin, and request foliage when its local base is ready. Ground the initial fallback at 8 m, subject to visual validation; its twenty files total only 331,000 compressed bytes. Avoid rendering a parent over its completed children: replace a whole sibling family atomically, or clip parent coverage to missing child regions. Retain fallback until replacement geometry has actually uploaded, not merely finished downloading.

For streaming-only work before mixed LOD, retain the terrain under unready areas and demand-load 1 m tiles. This saves close-view work but leaves distant buildings absent; the coarse city fallback makes the next stage visually complete. Do not call a view complete only when the entire city has loaded; expose “current view ready” and background activity separately.

Use screen-space error, not a fixed distance alone

SSE_pixels ≈ geometricError_metres × drawingBufferHeight
             / (2 × distanceToConservativeBounds × tan(verticalFov / 2))

reject outside pass frustum
keep coarse representation if error is acceptable
otherwise request/refine children, keeping parent until coverage is ready

The screen-space refinement principle is established in Cesium's tileset selection. Apply the principle to this existing grid; adopting Cesium or converting the project to 3D Tiles is unnecessary. Store content bounds, byte estimates, emptiness and hierarchy links in a small build-time manifest. A forest of 8 m roots naturally covers this map.

Use actual drawing-buffer height, distance to the nearest point of the bounds, and conservative handling when the camera is inside/intersects the near plane. At 1000 px and 28° FOV, a 1 m feature projects to about 2 px at 1 km and 0.5 px at 4 km. Projected width is not proof of invisibility: tall thin spires and lamps can remain recognizable. Preserve landmark silhouettes and night lights; keep a separate detail budget.

Do not equate error=s with a guaranteed error bound. Current downsampling uses occupancy thresholds and color selection, with thin-voxel exceptions at 2/4 m; it can remove or move surfaces. Start with conservative estimates and landmark locks, then precompute actual deviation bounds where practical. Test a tunable 1–2 px target, with a lower coarsening threshold than refining threshold and a short residence grace period. These are proposed starting values, not proven quality settings.

Neighbor levels need explicit seam coverage. Existing border walls keep independently loaded tiles closed but do not solve different-height surfaces between LODs. Require adjacent-level limits, retained boundary strips or skirts where visually safe; validate roads, bridges and tower bases. Coarse tiles already include foliage, so never overlay a separate 1 m foliage tile on its coarse parent.

Streaming mechanics and ownership

  • Use a desired-set generation number, tile content hash and bounded queues. Cancel obsolete fetches with AbortController; drop stale replies before allocating meshes. A cancel message cannot interrupt synchronous JS meshing until the worker yields, so first prevent stale queued jobs from starting.
  • Separate fetch/decompress, mesh and upload budgets. Bound jobs by estimated bytes as well as count. Up to twenty outstanding jobs with ten workers is not automatically appropriate for a memory-constrained browser.
  • Keep uploads within a measured per-frame byte/time budget. “Mesh ready” is not “GPU resident”; newly visible tiles otherwise bunch buffer uploads into one frame.
  • Use hysteretic LRU eviction; pin active replacements, the coarse fallback, shadow dependencies and navigation queries. Increment the world-data version on removal as well as addition to invalidate World.cache.
  • Handle fetch errors as retryable failures with bounded backoff. Current error-as-empty behavior must not remove the only fallback coverage. Confirmed empty tiles should be explicit manifest entries.
  • Separate query residency from render residency. Keep exact intervals near navigation, coarse coverage elsewhere, and request exact data for a distant pick if needed. World.solid currently treats missing positive-height data as empty; eviction must not silently turn an unknown building into free space. Long label rays also need coarse coverage or a defined unknown result.
  • Give shared GPU indices explicit ownership before adding eviction. Installed Three.js removes a geometry's index attribute on disposal, even if other geometries share it. Avoid deleting/reuploading the common index on each eviction, and audit old index generations when capacity grows.

HTTP caching should use content-versioned URLs or a dataset hash. The files are already gzip payloads decoded explicitly with DecompressionStream; avoid accidental double decompression by changing server Content-Encoding without coordinating the decoder. Do not bundle the whole city merely to reduce request count: it would defeat selective loading. Measure small spatial packs/range requests only if request overhead remains material after demand loading.

Compact geometry: a practical WebGL2 path

RepresentationAttribute bytes per quadSaving vs currentTradeoff
Current: four × (position 6 + attributes 4 + palette 2)48—Already much smaller than float position/normal/color.
Drop unused scale byte448.3%Small change, same vertex count.
Two Uint32 words per vertex3233.3%Bit unpacking and local origin; same triangle count.
One 16-byte record per instanced quad1666.7%Reconstruct four corners from origin, extents, orientation and AO.
Carefully packed 12-byte quad1275%More codec complexity; pursue only if 16-byte version wins.

The current Uint32 index pattern is shared across tiles: six indices mean 24 bytes per quad in the index sequence, but not another 24 bytes for every quad across the entire dataset. Count each actual shared buffer once. Instanced quads can use a single static four-corner/six-index unit quad.

A two-word vertex can fit this dataset without loss: local horizontal coordinates use 11 bits each (including the 1024 m endpoint), height 9 bits, palette 12, encoded axis/wall angle 8, AO 2, ground 8: 61 bits total. Assert ranges in the exporter and version the format; do not silently wrap if the map becomes taller. Use GLSL ES 3 unsigned attributes/bit shifts, correctly configured integer attribute binding, and a tile origin transform. Float attributes cannot losslessly carry an arbitrary packed 32-bit word. Integer input and instanced drawing are part of WebGL2's API specification.

For a 16-byte quad, a straightforward layout is five Uint16 values (local x/y/z and two extents), Uint16 palette, and four bytes for axis/wall angle, ground, four packed AO corners, and corner-order/flags. Keep the actual face axis separate from the facade's smoothed lighting direction. Preserve winding and AO-dependent diagonal. A 12-byte format can bit-pack these fields within the current coordinate bounds; 16 bytes is the simpler first prototype.

Confirmed offline: the audit implements that 16-byte record and reconstructs every position, palette, normal/wall-angle, AO and ground value for 344,010 quads across fifteen sample tiles. All comparisons pass; original emitted corner order is preserved. The unused scale byte is deliberately excluded. This confirms format feasibility, not browser shader throughput. Sample 1 m base records gzip to 42,336 / 117,370 / 67,791 bytes, still 7.8–8.1× their source columns; sampled 8 m records are 4.2–5.6× source size. Packing improves the precompute tradeoff but does not eliminate it.

Decode before all position-dependent shader work. Supply conservative CPU bounds without scanning reconstructed vertices. Update vVox to world coordinates and preserve facade/light-map alignment. Provide matching depth/shadow materials and verify N8AO's geometry path: teaching only the color shader to decode will produce wrong shadows and depth. Free CPU packed arrays only with an explicit context-loss rebuild path; the current null-after-upload behavior is not a reusable GPU source after context loss.

Binary Greedy Meshing demonstrates 8-byte quad records and vertex pulling, but its six-bit coordinate fields and simpler material representation do not fit our 128-cell, multi-scale, AO-bearing tiles directly. Its desktop indirect multidraw renderer is not a WebGL2 feature. Adapt the compact-record idea, not the API assumptions or native benchmark numbers.

Prefer instanced quad attributes for the first browser prototype. Texture-based pulling with integer textures and texelFetch is another route, but changes fetch behavior and material integration. Packing lowers bytes, not the number of rasterized faces; only LOD, meshing and culling remove that work.

Move static work offline where the bytes justify it

Precompute tight bounds, empty flags, interval query products, content hashes, detail ordering and per-tile byte/count metadata first. Bounds computed while emitting mesh vertices also avoid main-thread bounding-box/sphere scans without adding a second network product. Column tops can be gathered directly from run endpoints instead of rediscovered by scanning dense empty volume.

Do not immediately export the current expanded vertex arrays. Three selected 1 m base tiles produce gzip meshes of 73,390 / 208,479 / 119,895 bytes from input files of 5,219 / 15,094 / 8,329 bytes: 13.8–14.4× larger. Across all sampled levels and foliage, the comparison is similarly a material bandwidth tradeoff. Samples include median compressed size, largest size and tallest tile per level; they are a stress/representative sample, not a population-weighted estimate. CPU timings are Node timings with warm-up effects, not browser worker benchmarks.

Pilot premeshed compact quads for the twenty coarse fallback tiles, then compare gzip and a geometry codec against source columns plus runtime meshing. meshoptimizer's JavaScript/WebAssembly decoder supports compressed vertex/index data; evaluate it after quantization. Download compression and GPU representation are independent: a small download that expands back to 48 bytes per quad has not solved GPU residency.

For fine tiles, benchmark three paths: current gzip columns, offline compact quads, and a versioned local cache of derived meshes. Cache only if repeated visits amortize writes and storage; provide quota eviction and data/mesher-version invalidation. A shared mesher module used by build tooling and workers avoids maintaining subtly different AO and boundary implementations.

For runtime meshing retained as a fallback, zero-copy views can replace ArrayBuffer.slice at the current aligned offsets. Transfer the shared buffer once, not once per view, and do not retain a huge parent buffer merely to keep a tiny view. Dropping counts requires changing foliage input handling. Removing the final trim copy by transferring excess capacity trades allocation time for unused residency; benchmark that trade explicitly.

Lower priority: the lamp-map texture is generated from static lamp metadata on each boot and could be exported once if startup profiling justifies the asset. preserveDrawingBuffer=true is another A/B candidate, but the shadow-check harness reads pixels and needs an explicit capture path if this changes. Reducing AO/DPR can help fill-bound devices; it is a quality tradeoff, not the first response to excessive geometry. Idle rendering must respect animated lights, shader time, controls and pending uploads before it can safely stop.

Binary occupancy masks or a WASM mesher are later options if worker CPU remains limiting after reducing demand. JavaScript bitwise operations are 32-bit; native 64-bit mask code is not a drop-in translation. AO, ground values, palette and wall-angle distinctions currently constrain merging. Preserve those merge keys; the original voxel AO treatment explains why corner values and triangle diagonals matter. Do not remove internal border faces until neighbor/fallback coverage guarantees they are hidden.

Cull cheaply first; query occlusion only when it pays

Tighter bounds are the simplest next culling experiment. Three.js currently tests spheres; tall or elongated tiles can have loose spheres. Its installed Mesh intersectsFrustum hook is used by both color and shadow passes, so a tight box can be tested against the appropriate pass frustum without hiding offscreen casters globally. The research harness tests that with the existing world-space tile boxes. A local-origin implementation must transform bounds correctly.

A quadtree can reject whole regions and drive LOD/loading, but its benefit should be attributed primarily to avoiding work, not merely to replacing about a thousand cheap tests. Keep flat arrays for traversal state, propagate a frustum-plane mask, and traverse near-to-far. Only split expensive tiles into 32/64-cell or vertical subchunks if measured rejection exceeds extra draws, bounds and lost greedy merges. Draw chunk size and network tile size need not be the same.

Directional face batches can reject back-facing orientations before vertex processing, unlike raster back-face culling. However, six groups can multiply submissions; compare against packed instancing and measure the net result. Apply direction tests independently for camera and light. WEBGL_multi_draw can batch compatible draws when supported, with a normal draw-loop fallback. It is optional and does not add GPU-generated indirect draw commands.

An occlusion prototype worth testing

  1. After frustum/SSE selection, render reliable nearby opaque occluders front-to-back. Avoid a full duplicate depth pass unless it has a measured benefit.
  2. Query conservative bounds for expensive subtrees with ANY_SAMPLES_PASSED_CONSERVATIVE, depth testing enabled and color/depth writes disabled. Query boxes never become occluders.
  3. Poll availability in later frames. Never wait/spin for a same-frame result. The WebGL2 specification explicitly requires asynchronous result availability across event-loop turns.
  4. Render uncertain nodes. Do not reuse an “occluded” answer after camera/occluder changes without a conservative validity proof; on motion or disocclusion, invalidate and render until a fresh result exists. Retest hidden regions with a bounded schedule.
  5. Handle camera-inside and near-plane-intersecting bounds as visible. Keep occlusion state per view/pass; main-camera invisibility must not suppress shadows.

This follows the useful principle in CHC++: use hierarchy and temporal coherence to reduce query overhead. It is not an assumption that native CHC++ performance transfers to browser drivers. Tall tile boxes often contain substantial empty sky and whole-map views expose most roofs, so leaf-by-leaf queries may lose.

net saving = avoided vertex + fragment + draw-submission time
             − depth/proxy rendering − query submission/polling − traversal

Record query count, unresolved result latency, fraction of draws/triangles removed, CPU overhead and total GPU/frame p95. A proposed shipping gate is at least a 10% frame-time improvement in target occluded views without more than 3% regression in the open overview, across repeated runs on desktop and integrated/mobile GPUs. Those percentages are acceptance targets, not observed results.

Defer GPU Hi-Z culling: WebGL2 can build depth pyramids with render passes, but lacks core compute, SSBOs and indirect draw generation; vertex-shader rejection still submits vertices, and CPU readback risks synchronization. Software occlusion needs conservative coverage and sampling rules and competes with the main thread. Sparse voxel octree/DAG raycasting is an alternative renderer, not an incremental fix: it would have to replace the current lighting, shadows, AO and query integration. Mesh-shader and compute-driven voxel approaches are outside the WebGL2 target.

Implementation gates and reproducibility

Implement one change at a time and retain ?lod= as a diagnostic override. Measure settled overview, Civic Center, Bunker Hill and street-level views; a deterministic orbit/zoom/turn path; cold loading; repeated navigation with eviction; and night lighting. Track CPU submission, asynchronous GPU pass time, frame p50/p95, long tasks, time to useful view, fetch/decompress/mesh/upload timings, requested/aborted/wasted bytes, and unique resident CPU/GPU buffer estimates. GPU payload estimates are not whole-process RAM or driver residency.

Compare images at fixed camera/time and inspect near tile borders, roads, thin cables, palms, landmarks, AO diagonals and shadows from offscreen objects. Test rapid reversal, camera near/inside bounds, partial sibling loads, failed/cancelled requests, device-pixel-ratio changes and context restoration. For occupancy conversion, check ground/top/solid/raycast outputs against the original columns, including disjoint vertical runs and apron boundaries. For packing, round-trip every field at range boundaries and compare decoded positions and all rendering passes.

New evidence is limited to a desktop browser and offline data analysis. Coarse-level visual acceptability, packed-shader throughput, WAN behavior, adaptive LOD seams, eviction and occlusion gains remain unconfirmed; they are deliberately experiment-gated in this proposal.
