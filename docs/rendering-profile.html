DTLA rendering profile

November 2, 2026 · feat/rendering-optimization · initial baseline revision 91e7a53

Shadow-map redraws are the clearest optimization target. The initial profile below found substantial savings from reusing settled shadow maps. This PR now implements that reuse, with invalidation for camera/projection changes, light fitting and newly visible building or foliage meshes.

Implemented shadow optimization

The renderer skips shadow-map draws on unchanged frames. Daylight.invalidateShadow() opens both the renderer and light update gates. Camera position/orientation and projection changes invalidate even when the snapped light bounds stay the same; time/date changes invalidate through the light fit. City loading invalidates once per batch of newly visible meshes, including foliage, which does not update the retained-column version counter.

Paired implementation measurements average two six-second samples in reverse orders on the same hardware and viewport as the original profile. baseline now means the optimized viewer; always-shadows restores automatic redraws for comparison. Historical files retain their original meanings.

The browser integration check observes building and foliage loading batches, checks that each refreshes shadows before rendering, and verifies zero shadow draws on settled frames. It compares cached and forced-redraw images after pan, zoom, orbit, hour/date changes, night lighting and resize, including the first transition frame and scene rendering without postprocessing. Image tolerance is a mean channel difference below 0.2/255, with at least 99.9% of channels within 8/255; localized raster differences can occur. Future moving shadow casters must call the same invalidation method.

  • Shadows: 8192 × 8192 map. Civic Center submitted 1,575 draw calls/frame, dropping to 266 with the shadow map frozen. Bunker Hill dropped from 2,068 to 524. Thus 1,309 and 1,544 calls respectively were shadow redraws. These are total frame counts across composer passes, with renderer statistics reset once per composed frame.
  • Shadow fitting: about 0.005 ms CPU/frame while stationary. The existing cache works; fitting is not the expensive part. Renderer shadow auto-update remains enabled, so the cached fit still gets redrawn.
  • Labels: approximately 0.4–0.5 ms CPU/frame, including four occlusion raycasts, projection, sorting, placement and DOM writes. There is a CPU-saving opportunity, but this is not the primary frame-time bottleneck on this GPU.
  • Settled streaming updates: about 0.008–0.010 ms CPU/frame averaged over all frames. The 100 ms throttle already limits scanning; this figure does not describe meshing during loading.
  • Geometry: baseline totals were 24.9 million rendered triangles/frame in Civic Center and 31.3 million in Bunker Hill, including repeated geometry in shadows and supporting passes.

Overview, motion and night

The wide default overview measured 24.19 ms stationary and 24.32 ms during slow auto-orbit in six-second exploratory samples. Stationary p95 was 26.70 ms. It submitted 3,789 calls and 51.8 million triangles/frame; scene rendering including shadows took 22.68 ms on the GPU.

Bunker Hill at 19:30 measured 13.90 ms stationary and 14.50 ms during slow auto-orbit. Motion samples move through different viewpoints and are descriptive, not exact paired camera-path comparisons.

The repeated overview shadow comparison measured 23.15 → 18.63 ms/frame, a 19.5% reduction. Draw calls fell from 3,789 to 1,892 and rendered triangles from 51.8 million to 25.8 million. The remaining scene GPU time was about 17.52 ms: shadow invalidation alone will not bring this overview reliably below a 16.7 ms frame budget. The earlier 24.19 ms sample also illustrates ordinary run-to-run variation.

Tile loading and memory

  • At 1 m detail the viewer loads the entire map: 1,914 tile nodes, including foliage, producing 1,897 meshes. View-frustum priority affects loading order, not eventual retention.
  • Local instrumented loading took 20.4 s in Civic Center and 20.9 s in Bunker Hill after shader warm-up. The initial smoke run took 78 s. These are local Vite/browser startup observations, not production network benchmarks; shader cache, local disk and instrumentation affect them.
  • Worker concurrency reached six on this eight-thread CPU, then returned to one after the existing idle-retirement delay. Sampled aggregate worker JS heap usage plus ArrayBuffer backing storage peaked at about 127 MiB in Civic Center and 227 MiB in Bunker Hill. Sampling every few seconds can miss transient peaks.
  • The remaining idle worker retained about 15 MiB and 5 MiB respectively by the same measure. These are not whole-process RAM figures and exclude GPU memory.
  • 354.4 MiB of tile-column buffers remain on the main thread for ground queries, raycasts and collision data. Across all loaded meshes, attribute/index payload is approximately 599.2 MiB; this is a logical buffer total, not measured GPU residency. Offscreen geometry may still reside on the CPU, and texture/render-target/driver allocations are additional.

Recommended implementation order

  1. Shadow invalidation is implemented. The original frozen-shadow experiment was an upper-bound diagnostic; the implementation preserves refreshes during camera motion and tile loading.
  2. Investigate view-dependent geometry and retention. The overview remains geometry-heavy, and all 1 m tiles stay resident. Measure distant detail reductions and buffer retention before choosing a larger streaming redesign.
  3. Let stationary labels settle, then stop repeating work. Invalidate on camera/viewport changes, tile data, fonts, selected pins and UI obstacles. Preserve the 150 ms label-settle delay and complete pending occlusion checks.
  4. Keep visual effects by default. AO is the largest postprocessing cost here. Bloom, depth blur and SMAA are comparatively small; any quality reduction should be justified with visual comparisons and a slower-device profile.

Other overrides: URL, CHROME, HOUR, SAMPLE_MS. The harness exposes instances by modifying the served Vite module response and wraps methods in the test page only. It does not modify viewer source or shipping defaults. It depends on the current internal instance/pass structure.

After loading, the harness waits five seconds, warms each variant for 1.5 seconds and samples for six seconds. CPU method timings include submission overhead; GPU passes use asynchronous EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2 queries. CPU and GPU timings overlap and must not be added together. GPU queries marked disjoint are discarded. Sub-millisecond CPU values have limited timer precision. Per-pass differences are not strictly additive because the GPU workload changes.

The raw shadowDrawCalls and shadowSubmitCpuMs counters are per shadow-render invocation, including no-op invocations from supporting passes. To obtain a per-frame mean, multiply their mean by their sample count and divide by composerCpuMs.n. Passes 0–4 correspond to the GPU table above.
